Patek Philippe × The Hour Glass

The private room,
at scale

Strategic brief for the design team — March 2026

By Gael and Erik — AKQA / Aleph Labs
Background

The Hour Glass is the authorised Patek Philippe retailer across Asia-Pacific — nine dedicated boutiques spanning Singapore, Kuala Lumpur, Sydney, Bangkok, and beyond. Patek Philippe sits within the Holy Trinity of watchmaking. Family-owned. Pure mechanical. Entry price: SGD 30,000+. Collector pieces reach into the millions. No sale without a relationship.

The Hour Glass doesn't just adhere to that ethos — it amplifies it. Watch enthusiasts serving watch enthusiasts. Every specialist on the floor speaks the same language as the collector across the table.

THG approached AKQA because of the Hermès website. Michael Tay, Group Managing Director, wants to see what happens when a network that understands luxury at that level builds the digital presence for the most exclusive watch brand on earth. He will review and approve all creative direction on March 23.

Nine boutiques. Nine independent URLs — same structure per site. One chance to set the standard.

The insight

The new luxury isn't what you show — it's what you withhold. And it's because of what you withhold that you begin to speak a coded language. A Patek Philippe on the wrist isn't a status symbol — it's a recognition signal. You either read the dial or you don't. It is the ultimate expression of taste — belonging not through display, but through discernment.

Patek Philippe is the purest expression of mechanical watchmaking. No quartz. No compromise. The Hour Glass is the partner who not only understands that purity but lives it — through its people, its operations, its obsession with craft. The digital experience must carry that same honesty forward.

The tension

The world's most considered watch brand has the most unconsidered digital presence.

Every Patek boutique website today is a brochure — compliant, forgettable, identical. It treats a $500,000 collector the same as a passing browser. The boutique would never do this. The website does it every time.

The opportunity isn't to redesign. It's to whisper luxury at the most extreme level. Not louder. More honest.

The ask

Design a digital experience worthy of the private room. Nine boutique websites that feel like being invited in — not marketed to. One design system, three visitor modes: the researcher, the connoisseur, the collector. Same room. Different lighting.

Prove it with a working prototype, not a deck.

Present to Michael Tay: March 23, 2026

The audience

These are the people at Art Basel, not the art fair. The ones whose Rolls-Royce was presented to them at an airshow, because that's where you find them. They notice when a typeface has been compromised, when a material has been substituted, when an experience has been templated. They don't browse — they appraise.

But most importantly: they are enthusiasts. They care about movements, about finishing, about the difference between a Geneva Seal and a Hallmark of Geneva. The best way to earn their attention is to demonstrate that you understand what they understand. Watch enthusiasts serving watch enthusiasts — that's the standard online, just as it is in the boutique.

The journey

The website is one moment in a longer relationship. The strategy will address the full arc across three directions:

Direction 1
Exceptional taste

When a human lands on the site, it must be unmistakably, unambiguously global luxury. Not aspirational. Not "premium." The kind of restraint and precision that signals to the visitor: this was made by people who understand what you understand. The website is the first proof that this boutique is worthy of your time.

Direction 2
The digital concierge

Drawing on AKQA's Generative Store thinking (ref: AKQA × Cartier Generative Store), the site adapts to who is looking — surfacing the right collections, the right depth, the right invitation at the right moment. No chatbot. No visible AI. The page itself is the intelligence.

Direction 3
Discovery to relationship — GEO through to CRM

The full pipeline. From Answer Engine Optimisation to adaptive concierge intake to AI-generated client briefs pushed directly into The Hour Glass CRM — so the watch specialist walks into the appointment already knowing. The relationship begins before the handshake.

The playground
Play

An end-to-end luxury experience — from how a collector discovers the boutique, through the website that earns their attention, to the concierge moment that begins the relationship, to the CRM integration that makes the boutique team's first conversation feel like a second. Generative personalisation. AI-powered concierge. Micro-interactions. Editorial storytelling. Collection as narrative, not catalogue.

Can't

No e-commerce, no pricing, no stock, no promotions, no smartwatches. All imagery from PPE DAM. Strict brand guidelines. "Concierge" — never "reservation."

The territory
Hermès. Aman Resorts. A Collected Man. Louis Vuitton.

The thread: restraint as authority. Breathing room. Photography that makes you feel temperature.

Michael Tay sees the prototype and says: "This is the first time anyone has made our digital feel like our boutique."

The Market

Asia Pacific is the centre of the watch world.

Asia Pacific commands 41.58% of the global luxury watch market — the single largest share of any region. The market grew from $25.06B to $28.07B between 2025 and 2026 alone, at a 7.1% annual growth rate. The 9 boutiques in this brief are not distributed across peripheral markets. They sit at the centre of the fastest-growing collector ecosystem on earth.

The Brief, Reframed

The ask is "9 websites."

The real ask is 9 distinct expressions of a singular relationship — between a collector, a timepiece, and the people who curate that connection.

Nine independent URLs. Same structure per site. But each one must feel like entering a specific room, in a specific city, with a specific character. Not a template with swapped photography. A considered expression of place.

The Gap

The luxury digital gap is real — and it has a cost.

Fewer than half of luxury consumers under 40 report being fully satisfied with the digital experience of luxury brands. For a brand operating at Patek Philippe's tier, this gap is not neutral. Every digital touchpoint that underperforms quietly erodes the relationship before it begins.

The watches are irreproachable. The service is unmatched. The digital experience is where the standard breaks.

Closing this gap is not a design exercise. It is brand stewardship.

The Central Idea

Nine rooms.
One philosophy.

A Patek Philippe boutique is not a shop — it's a private world you're invited into. The relationship begins before the door opens. These sites operate the same way: not a catalogue of watches, but the moment before the encounter.

No stock information — the boutique holds that. The concierge appointment is the primary CTA — the door is the product. Each site reflects its specific location — you're entering this room, in this city.

The Collector

The relationship begins long before the first appointment.

A serious collector spends months — sometimes years — researching before their first boutique visit. They already know the reference numbers. They understand the complications. They have formed a view of what it means to own one.

The website is where that journey begins. It is not a product catalogue. It is the first conversation with someone who may become a client for life — and whose children may become clients after them.

Design for the collector. The first-time buyer will follow.

The Architecture of Difference

Nine boutiques. Nine distinct characters. The logic of differentiation — what makes Bangkok different from Sydney, what makes Malmaison different from Raffles Arcade.

Consistent

Design system. Navigation patterns. Typographic scale. Booking flow. Brand compliance layer.

Distinct

Photography. Editorial voice. Colour palette. Cultural references. Boutique-specific events and collections.

Design Approach

Luxury in digital design is defined less by what is present than by what is deliberately absent. No urgency, no noise, no signals that optimise for conversion over experience. What remains — typography, pace, the behaviour of light on a surface, the weight of a transition — must carry the full weight of the brand.

The sites we are proposing are built on a clear distinction: the content is Patek Philippe's — the collections, the imagery, the brand voice. The craft is ours — how that content is held, revealed, and felt. Our role is not to decorate the brand. It is to give it a room worthy of what it contains.

Selected clients
Our Work
Hermès

La Maison Des Carrés

A store like no other.

An icon of French luxury fashion, Hermès silk is unique in its fantasy and playfulness. To house this vibrantly creative world, the conventional e-commerce experience needed to be reimagined – moving beyond purchase to a total immersion in the artistry of the brand. An approach free enough to combine the artistry and craftsmanship of La Maison des Carrés with the pragmatism of a clear and intuitive e-commerce presentation of more than 600 products.

Always welcoming and informative, perfect customer service combines with delightful surprises unrestrained by the real world. Entirely illustrated yet fully responsive, the ever-changing Maison des Carrés is available on any device and open in 27 countries across Europe, Asia and America.

The worldwide coverage includes articles in Elle, Vogue, Creative Review, New York Post, WWD Japan, Le Figaro and Creativity.

Montblanc

Timepieces

Part biography. Part Chronography.

Montblanc had the opportunity to create an experience where the micro-detailing of the watches could be interrogated and explored by their audience with High Definition touch response screens.

The Timepieces application combines the beauty of a coffee table book with the technology of retina displays and multi-touch navigation to put their watches in the hands of their audience. Over 40 watches from 6 timeless collections could be inspected by the viewer in the same detail as the watchmaker himself. Users can virtually "try on" their favourites by measuring their wrists in app and seeing how the watches look on their arm.

The Montblanc Timepieces App launched with international acclaim, being awarded Mobile Site Of The Day by the FWA.

Bvlgari

New rules

How to be a bold, Bvlgari woman.

New Year is typically a time to reflect upon yourself. Better yourself. To insist on resolutions that are at best restorative, and at worst tired. But no one ever said bettering yourself had to be a chore — especially not the bold Bvlgari woman.

Inspired by the defining letter of the Bvlgari logo, RVLES were devised to liberate, not dictate. A call to women to use the year ahead to revel in excess. A striking visual language of hand gestures represents each RVLE, punctuated with impassioned copy urging women to 'leave nothing to chance' and 'grab life by the jewels'.

The opening chapter of Bvlgari's new visual approach on Instagram saw engagement and followers increase. The self-assured mindset of RVLES in topical follow-up campaigns for Valentine's Day and Chinese New Year added a further 300,000 new followers in six weeks.

Memento / Malaysia

Crafting Perfect Moments

Time is transformed.

We believe that jewellery is not just an accessory; it's a reflection of life's milestones and a testament to enduring bonds. Through "Craft Perfect Moments", we help you celebrate these moments with jewellery that is beautifully crafted, uniquely yours, and designed to be treasured for generations to come.

A digital platform built around life's defining moments — not categories, not collections. The experience guides visitors through a narrative of milestones: engagements, anniversaries, the first gift a parent gives a child. Each piece of jewellery is presented not as a product, but as a future memory. Bespoke consultation flows replace the conventional product filter.

The platform aims to drive a significant uplift in consultation bookings and extended average session time. The "Craft Perfect Moments" brand platform will become the foundation for all subsequent seasonal campaigns.
